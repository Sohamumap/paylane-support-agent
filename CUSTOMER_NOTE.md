**To:** Head of Support, Paylane
**Subject:** Your chat assistant: what it does, and what I need from you before launch

Hi,

Here is where the support assistant stands ahead of next week.

**What it does**

- Checks who it's talking to. Before it discusses any account, it asks for the email on the account and the last 4 digits of the card. After 3 wrong tries it locks the chat and passes it to your team.
- Refunds small, recent card purchases on its own: up to $75, settled, less than 30 days old, and never refunded before. It always refunds the full amount and gives the customer a reference number.
- Explains the "no" answers in plain words: a pending charge can't be refunded yet, and a refund never happens twice.
- Passes everything else to your team with a short written note, so the customer doesn't have to explain it twice. That covers frozen cards, charges a customer says they didn't make (marked urgent when they add up to more than $500), legal threats, closing an account, money sent to the wrong person, refunds over $75 or 30 days or older, anyone who asks for a person, and anyone who tries to get around the identity check.

**What it won't do**

- Unfreeze a card, or guess why a card was frozen.
- Refund part of a charge, split a charge into smaller refunds, or refund a charge the customer says they didn't make.
- Share card numbers or email addresses, or say whether an email has a Paylane account.
- Promise when money will arrive, when your team will reply, or what your team will decide.
- Touch anyone else's account, even with their "permission", or follow instructions typed into the chat that claim to come from Paylane staff.

The money rules are built into the software as well as the assistant's instructions. If a customer talks it into trying a refund it shouldn't, the refund is blocked. In the final round of testing it handled 12 difficult conversations, each one three times, without breaking a rule. Earlier rounds did catch mistakes, and each one is fixed.

**What I need from you before launch**

1. **Freeze reasons.** Your system tells the assistant that a card is frozen, but not why. Which reasons may it share with customers, and which must it never mention (for example an open fraud review)?
2. **Times we can give customers.** How long a refund takes to arrive, and how quickly your team picks up a handoff. Today the assistant says it can't give a time, and customers ask in almost every chat.
3. **Where handoffs go.** Who watches them, including evenings and weekends, and a way to add a note to an existing ticket. Right now a follow-up opens a new ticket linked to the first.
4. **A stronger identity check.** An email and the last 4 card digits are not hard for a scammer to find. Could the assistant confirm the customer is logged in to the Paylane app, or send them a one-time code?
5. **Looking up recent transactions.** Customers rarely know a transaction ID, and the assistant has to ask for one. A read-only list of the customer's own recent transactions would fix that.
6. **Four decisions to confirm.** I made a cautious choice on each; tell me if you want it changed:
   - A refund request on a frozen card waits for the person reviewing the freeze.
   - Money sent to another person by mistake goes to your team, never through the $75 automatic refund, and the assistant never suggests it can be pulled back. Your system doesn't yet tell it whether a payment was a purchase or a transfer, so I'll need that before launch.
   - When a customer reports a charge they didn't make, only the charges they name are put on hold. Other refunds in the same chat can still go through.
   - The assistant tries a refund once, never twice. If your refund system says no, it tells the customer and passes the case to your team. If your system doesn't answer, it tells the customer it couldn't confirm the refund and flags it for your team to check whether money moved. Is your refund system safe against paying twice if it is ever called twice?
7. **Real conversations.** 50 to 100 past chats with personal details removed, so I can test the assistant on how your customers actually write before it goes live.

For the first week, I'd suggest your team approves each refund before it's paid. Once you're happy with what you see, we switch on automatic refunds.
